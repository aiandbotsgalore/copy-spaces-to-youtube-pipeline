# VERDICT: REVISE

### 1. Core Narrative Diagnosis

The locked Prologue and Act I establish a gripping, intimate audio documentary with a clear investigative mission: examining how a self-described trauma advocate built deep personal trust in late-night audio rooms, collected her confidantes’ private vulnerabilities, and turned those confidences into weapons once relationships fractured. However, the moment the script crosses into **Act II: The Archive**, the storytelling voice suffers from severe verification paralysis. Clearly chastened by the recent fact-checking pass, Logan’s narration retreats from active investigative storytelling into repetitive, defensive compliance notes (*"We have his testimony, not independent records..."*, *"That is Cambian's account..."*, *"She wasn't offering a police record..."*, *"Those are Timmy's allegations..."*, *"Again, those are allegations from a former friend, not a finding"*). In an audio medium, hearing the host recite variations of the same legalistic disclaimer after eight consecutive clips in Scene 4 alone drains the human tension out of the room and turns a tragedy about betrayed trust into an anxious, clinical memo.

Structurally and chronologically, Acts II through IV also contain visible editing scars and medium-breaking errors that will confuse a listener. Database jargon from the verification pass has leaked directly into the script (*"In February-tagged recordings"*), narration instructs an audio-only audience to *"Read the wording carefully"*, Scene 7 contains an accidental double-printed `**LOGAN**` header and duplicate sentence, and the inline voice directions for text messages (`[plain neutral delivery, no imitation of source speaker]`) directly contradict the script header’s promise of *"dedicated AI voice recreations."* Worse, the chronology around late September is disorienting: in Scene 4, Logan introduces a clip where he is warmly cheering Angela on as a *"September 26 conversation"*—without explaining how that timestamp relates to the September 8–18 war—and in Scene 7, Logan audits his own past actions in bizarre third-person bureaucracy (*"I haven't independently examined the underlying file for this documentary, and that claim about its contents remains my account"*).

The path to a standout broadcast script does not require sacrificing a single ounce of evidentiary rigor. Every boundary established in your verification notes—separating unproven third-party claims from first-person admissions, preserving Angela’s sympathetic explanations for the welfare check, and distinguishing the 6:35 AM CPS message from the 6:39 AM civil-damages message—must stay. But those distinctions need to be woven into propulsive, character-driven audio narration. By consolidating repetitive third-party caveats, clarifying the three-stage escalation of leaks against Sav, letting emotional peaks breathe without caption-like summaries, and restoring thematic weight to the Epilogue, the second half of the show will match the power of its opening.
