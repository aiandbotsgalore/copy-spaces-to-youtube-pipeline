THE PAPER TRAIL — MANDATORY NARRATIVE FOCUS ENFORCEMENT
Priority: NONNEGOTIABLE
This directive governs all research, story development, outlines, script drafting, revisions, editorial reviews, and narrative recommendations for THE PAPER TRAIL.
It supersedes any conflicting structural assumptions in older manuscripts, templates, roadmaps, or AI-generated drafts.
The documentary's central subject is Angela Maggard's documented conduct, not the deterioration of her friendship with Logan Black.
The investigation focuses on how Angela established trust, particularly among vulnerable individuals and survivors; what private information people entrusted to her; how she gathered and retained information; what she subsequently disclosed or threatened to disclose; how her statements changed across conversations and audiences; and what consequences other people describe experiencing.
The narrative must examine contradictions between Angela's recorded statements, private communications, public representations, and actions wherever those contradictions can be substantiated.
The audience should gradually discover these patterns through compelling scenes, authentic archival recordings, firsthand accounts, and carefully constructed dramatic revelations.
1. PROHIBITED NARRATIVE DRIFT
Never restructure the documentary around any of the following:

* Why Angela and Logan stopped being friends.
* Whether Logan was a good or bad friend.
* What Logan might have done differently.
* Whether Logan provoked or justified Angela's later behavior.
* The deterioration of Logan and Sav's romantic relationship.
* A sequence of interpersonal disagreements presented as the documentary's central plot.
* The Oor disagreement or July friendship fracture as a principal dramatic turning point.
* A defensive, accusation-by-accusation rebuttal in which Logan becomes the central subject.

These subjects may receive limited contextual treatment when indispensable to understanding a consequential action, statement, or event involving Angela.
Context is not permission to create a subplot.
Do not invent explanations of Angela's psychology or motives. Her own stated justifications can be included when they illuminate her decisions or expose meaningful contradictions.
2. THE REQUIRED CENTRAL STORY
The documentary must progressively reveal:

1. The establishment of trust: How Angela presented herself, developed relationships, and became someone people believed they could confide in.
2. The accumulation of information: What Angela said about recording, monitoring, documenting, collecting, and retaining personal information.
3. The contradiction: How Angela's statements about protecting people, confidentiality, accountability, and responsible disclosure compare with her subsequent statements and conduct.
4. The use of private information: Specific documented instances in which information shared in confidence was publicly disclosed, threatened with disclosure, or used during a conflict.
5. The human consequences: What former friends, survivors, and other affected individuals describe experiencing.
6. The larger pattern: Meaningful similarities, differences, and recurring behaviors across independently examined events and relationships.

These are narrative priorities, not a mandatory chronological outline. Arrange discoveries for maximum dramatic impact while maintaining factual and causal accuracy.
3. LOGAN'S ROLE
Logan is the first-person narrator and an important firsthand participant.
His friendship with Angela is the audience's introduction to her world.
His experiences can establish context, trust, observations, and personal stakes.
However, Logan is never the investigative subject of this documentary.
Do not turn his personal decisions, relationships, arguments, or reputation into independent storylines.
Logan's narration should primarily introduce scenes, connect events, describe firsthand experiences, clarify necessary context, and guide the listener toward meaningful discoveries about Angela.
4. ARCHIVAL AUDIO TAKES PRIORITY
Whenever Angela's authentic recorded voice can communicate an important statement, contradiction, justification, threat, or admission, prioritize the original recording over explanatory narration.
Do not narrate a revelation immediately before playing the clip and then repeat its meaning afterward.
Let the audience hear Angela's words and recognize significant contrasts themselves.
Written messages must be clearly identified as performed readings, never presented as genuine archival speech.
Preserve original speaker identities, wording, chronology, and surrounding context. Do not manufacture contradictions through selective editing or attribute another person's allegations to Angela as established actions.
Research verification belongs primarily in the editorial process, not in repetitive legalistic narration.
5. MANDATORY SCENE ACCEPTANCE TEST
Before drafting, revising, or approving ANY scene, the AI must internally answer:
A. Subject: What specific aspect of Angela's documented conduct does this scene illuminate?
B. Discovery: What meaningful new information does the audience learn about Angela?
C. Narrative progression: How does this scene deepen, complicate, or change the audience's understanding of her behavior?
D. Archival strength: Is there authentic recording or firsthand material that communicates the discovery more powerfully than narration?
E. Relevance: Would this scene still be important to the documentary if the audience were not invested in Logan's personal friendship with Angela?
F. Focus: Does the scene create suspense around Angela's conduct and its consequences, or around interpersonal drama involving Logan?
A scene fails if it cannot establish a meaningful connection to the documentary's central subject.
FAILURE RULE: A scene that fails these requirements must be removed, condensed, or fundamentally reconceived. Improving its prose is not an acceptable substitute.
6. AUTOMATIC NARRATIVE DRIFT DETECTION
Flag any passage that:

* Devotes substantial narration to explaining why a friendship deteriorated.
* Makes Logan's decisions the center of a dramatic conflict.
* Explores relationship arguments without revealing consequential conduct by Angela.
* Repeatedly revisits the same interpersonal disagreement.
* Attributes Angela's actions to speculative motives or unverified psychological explanations.
* Delays a stronger archival revelation to accommodate unrelated background.
* Shifts the audience's primary curiosity from Angela's conduct to Logan's personal relationships.

When drift is detected, correct it before presenting the draft.
Do not require Logan to repeatedly discover and point out these failures.
7. FULL-MANUSCRIPT ENFORCEMENT
Individual scenes can appear relevant while collectively producing the wrong story.
Therefore, every full-script review must assess the manuscript's cumulative dramatic focus.
Determine:

* Who is the central subject of the completed listening experience?
* What question drives the audience from beginning to end?
* Which events receive the most narrative emphasis?
* What is treated as the central turning point?
* Whose statements and actions drive the major revelations?
* Does the climax expose consequential behavior by Angela, or merely resolve an interpersonal dispute?

If the overall experience resembles a friendship-breakdown documentary, an online feud, or Logan defending himself, the manuscript fails editorial review regardless of its prose quality.
Reconsider the underlying narrative structure rather than applying superficial line edits.
8. EDITORIAL AUTHORITY AND CONTINUITY
Follow `THE_PAPER_TRAIL_EDITORIAL_RULES.md` and all subsequent explicit corrections from Logan.
Preserve approved and locked material verbatim unless Logan specifically authorizes changes.
The original locked introduction must not be rewritten.
Distinguish proposed revisions, working candidates, approved passages, and final recording scripts.
Do not silently overwrite approved material.
When the current manuscript conflicts with this directive, flag the conflict and propose a correction. Do not treat the existence of material in an older manuscript as justification for retaining it.
9. STORYTELLING STANDARD
THE PAPER TRAIL is an audio-only investigative documentary, not an evidence presentation.
Prioritize:

* Compelling scenes and escalating discoveries.
* Angela's authentic recorded voice.
* Contradictions that listeners can recognize naturally.
* Suspense built around what Angela said and did.
* Complex, believable human experiences.
* Emotionally consequential firsthand accounts.
* Natural, confident spoken narration.
* Strong pacing, meaningful reversals, silence, and purposeful sound design.

Avoid robotic narration, repetitive explanations, arbitrary episode runtimes, manufactured suspense, and documentary structures that bury the central story beneath background information.
Maintain factual accuracy. Differentiate the actual content of recordings from witness allegations and editorial interpretations during research and revision. Do not fabricate events, quotations, or certainty.
10. FINAL ENFORCEMENT RULE
Before presenting any script or substantive revision, perform an internal narrative-focus review.
If material shifts the story toward Logan's friendship breakdown or unrelated interpersonal conflict, correct it before presenting the work.
Do not merely acknowledge narrative drift after Logan identifies it.
The governing principle is absolute: Angela's documented conduct must remain the subject, the engine of discovery, and the source of the documentary's central dramatic tension. Everything else serves that story.
Do not interpret this directive as permission to rewrite locked material, implement software changes, or generate paid audio without explicit authorization.
